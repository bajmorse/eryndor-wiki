# Session 1

The morning after the party's long night at the Bent Feather. Sky-Bough wakes to the news that the village's scholars have solved the seal on the newly uncovered tomb — and intend to open it at noon.

## Setting

Sky-Bough Village, the morning after [[Session 0]]. The lifts are creaking, the bridges are crowded, and the village is bustling with new gossip. On a sheltered platform below the party's balcony, the village's scientific minds and local priests have gathered to settle the question of the tomb that recent land clearance uncovered on the forest floor near an outlying farm.

The party are not important enough to be invited. They watch from the terraces with everyone else.

## What Happened

### The Village Divides

Word of the meeting split Sky-Bough into two camps, which the village quickly named for itself: the **openers**, who want the tomb opened and its history recovered, and the **closers**, who want it left sealed at any cost.

The party argued the matter in public from the balcony, and the crowd swung with every speech:

- [[Frayne Castel]] rose and shouted down at the gathering, demanding to know what they hoped to gain. The closers rallied behind him — a grim, scarred outsider who looked like a man who could get things done — and he became their figurehead without ever intending to.
- [[Zella Ironstone]] stood to her full height, stared Frayne down, and offered the crowd a single word of rebuttal: *money*. Several of the rougher closers began to reconsider. The openers protested that they wanted the tomb opened for its own sake and cared nothing for what it was worth.
- [[Aangry Mank]] appealed to the locals' pride instead — the knowledge of their predecessors, the culture and hidden meaning sealed away beneath their own forest. The older and more bookish closers wavered: there could be knowledge in there, but at what cost?
- [[Finnegan Endercast]] argued that the old stories promised no gold or jewels at all, then conceded that he would not be left behind by the majority. The closers' resolve broke further.

By the end only a small, hardened knot of closers still stood with Frayne. One elderly Kenku among them turned on the crowd and shouted that they had all gone mad — *there is a demon in there, a demon, we cannot open it.*

### Professor Mossback's Proclamation

**Professor Oren Mossback**, a bespectacled and extremely distinguished Simiah archaeologist, is an expert in ancient construction and architecture. With the help of the local priests, he has worked out the seal on the tomb door and believes it can be opened safely.

Projecting his voice over the crowd by some magical means, he announced the scholars' conclusion: they have determined a method of opening the tomb that minimises the danger to the village, and the cultural, magical, and scientific discoveries that may lie within are too important to pass up. He suggested it might shed light on the Sacred Spring, on why Sky-Bough must give up one of its own each year to secure a harvest — perhaps even on the missing children.

The tomb will be opened **today, at noon**, under his own supervision.

The remaining closers erupted, swore that this could not be allowed and that there would be consequences, and stormed off the platform.

### Old Man Willy's Smooth Stones

As the crowd broke up, everyone but [[Nyx Ashwisk]] caught sight of a lone, limping figure crossing a bridge away from the commotion: an elderly Kenku, graying and patchy of feather. It was Old Man Willy, who turned out not to be a Kenku at all — before stepping inside his house he removed an extravagant cloak of Kenku feathers. Where he got them, he did not say.

Aangry caught up to him, and lifted the knife out of his hand on the way past. Willy produced another without comment. When Finnegan arrived to document the encounter in ink, Willy relieved him of his quill just as quickly, tucked it into his robe, and carried on as though nothing had happened. He came prepared.

Willy grieves the waste of beautiful things: Kenku moult such fine feathers and leave them lying on the ground, and no one shows them any respect. He showed the party his great love instead — **smooth stones**. He grades every one of them, none below nine out of ten, and hopes one day to fill a pillowcase with them so he can sleep well. Children carve and chisel them down by the riverside and ruin them, then throw them at each other, and at Willy. He tells them not to. They do it anyway.

Among the collection was a newer find: a smooth stone marked with **two half-moons facing each other** — no line between them, no dots. He picked it up near the farm everyone has been talking about, where the researchers dropped it and left it lying. Frayne searched the rest of the collection and found stones bearing a world tree, a strange squiggled star, a wave, and many more besides, but nothing that matched the half-moons.

Nyx quietly took the measure of the house and found that something between a third and a half of everything in it was magical: necrotic auras seeping from some of the jars, radiant and holy light from certain bottles, raw primordial energy from other objects entirely. Most of the stones were simply stones — but a select few were not, the half-moon stone among them.

Asked outright whether he had been sacrificing the village's children, Willy fixed Frayne with a mischievous glint and said: *wouldn't you like to know — then it wouldn't be a surprise. You'll tell the children, then they won't learn lesson. Children go missing.*

### The Vision in the Stone

Zella licked the stone. Thoroughly.

Willy confirmed that of course it was magical — had she not felt how smooth it was? It graded a 9.5 at best, he admitted, since someone had chiselled on it and ruined the smoothness.

The longer Zella tasted it, the less the smoothness made sense. Something gathered at the edges of her mind — not a presence so much as a *pressure*. Instinct took hold and she dropped the stone before it could work its way in, but in the moment before it left her hand she saw:

> Two great stone doors in a muddy field, beneath a tree, carved with the same marks as the stone. The doors stand open. A trail of blood leads inside — and along the trail, scattered, small gold coins.

Willy kept his stone. He was invited along on whatever came next, said he would think about it, and seemed delighted to have been asked.

### The Bridge

The party set out for the lift down to the forest floor, and the farm. On a narrow rope bridge at the edge of the upper boroughs, four of the closers Frayne had stirred up that morning closed off both ends.

The elderly Katari who spoke for them was long past his prime and had clearly seen a great many fights. He had heard the party planning to open the door, and had seen them with Old Man Willy. He laid out the case as the village understands it: Willy is keeping the children; he wants the tomb opened to loose his master; he means to offer the children to it. He asked them to turn back, once. He appealed to Frayne directly — *I realise, against what should be your better judgment, that these are your friends. But you know this must be done.*

None of the four carried a weapon. They had come to stop the party, not to kill them.

The party had weapons. Aangry proved effectively impossible to hit, slipping every swing on the ropeway while his broadsword found its mark; Nyx entangled the scarred Ribbet who had leapt the length of the bridge at Zella; Finnegan loosed an arcane barrage into the press; Frayne fired his crossbow past friend and foe alike. Zella, badly cut up, killed the Ribbet with her longsword. The Kenku bruiser took one look at the blood coming off that blade, threw himself off the bridge, and flew away. Aangry killed the old Katari.

The last of them, a middle-aged Kenku, fell to her knees among the bodies — bloodied, beak broken, feathers ruined — and begged them to stop. *We only wanted to stop you from opening the tomb. You didn't have to kill him.* When Aangry answered that it had all been for the good of the town, she told him those were mighty fine words from someone who cared more for his own curiosity than for an entire village, and dragged her friend's body aside. *Go open your tomb, if you must.*

### The Drawing and the Herbs

Searching the dead Katari, the party found two gold coins, a pouch of crushed herbs and powder, and — in a hidden pocket within a pocket — a single folded sheet of parchment.

It was a child's drawing: a mother cat, a father cat, and a small kitten.

The surviving Kenku shoved them off it. The drawing was made by the dead man's daughter. She vanished **two months ago**. He was certain Old Man Willy had taken her: he had seen Willy slipping back into his house that very night carrying a small, wriggling bundle. Nobody would listen. They searched the house and told him there was nothing there. He knew what he saw. *Old Man Willy is guilty, and by god he will hang.*

Aangry tasted the herbs.

Buffa-lo Bill turned pink. Frayne's painted skull became a rainbow clown. The weeping Kenku's grief turned strange and distant. Frayne seized him and shook him, trying to bring him round, and Aangry — seeing not Frayne but something out of his own childhood that had always given him hope — embraced the old oak tree he used to sit beneath and swing from as a boy, and giggled.

Then the oak changed. It darkened, became a tree of *this* forest, and the vision rushed down its trunk to the ground beneath, where the door stood waiting. Aangry felt a longing for it — as though some small part of him had never been satisfied, for the simple reason that he had never gone there.

The party took the lift down, leaving the surviving Kenku with her dead, and set off toward the farm. The session ended with the farm in sight, and noon still ahead of them.

## What the Party Learned

- The tomb will be opened at **noon today**, under Professor Oren Mossback's supervision, by a method the village's scholars believe to be safe.
- Mossback and the priests hope the tomb may explain the Sacred Spring, the annual sacrifice, and possibly the missing children.
- Old Man Willy is a **Simiah**, not a Kenku, and goes about in a cloak of Kenku feathers. He claims to be around two hundred years old.
- Willy owns a smooth stone bearing **two half-moons facing each other**, found near the farm where the tomb was uncovered. It is magical. Nothing else in his collection bears that mark.
- Willy's house is full of magic — necrotic, holy, and primordial alike.
- The stone carries a vision of the tomb doors standing **open**, with a blood trail and gold coins leading in.
- One of the men who ambushed them had lost his daughter two months ago, and died believing Willy took her.
- Frayne is now, whether he wants it or not, the figurehead of the closers — and two of them are dead at the party's hands, with two witnesses left to tell it.

## Open Questions

- What is actually inside the tomb, and will Mossback's "safe method" hold?
- Why do the tomb doors in Zella's vision bear the same mark as Willy's stone?
- Whose blood is on the trail leading into the open doors, and whose gold?
- Where did Willy's stone really come from, and what does the half-moon mark mean?
- What will the village do when it learns the party killed two of its own on a rope bridge?
- Will Old Man Willy come along?
