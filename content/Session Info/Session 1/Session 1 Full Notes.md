# Session 1 Full Notes

Reference material from Session 1: the people, factions, and objects the party encountered, and where matters stand going into the next session. For the account of events in order, see [[Session 1]].

## New Faces

### Professor Oren Mossback

A Simiah scholar of archaeology, and specifically of ancient construction and architecture. Bespectacled, formal, and carries himself with enormous dignity. He is the one who worked out the seal on the tomb door, with help from the local priests, and he is the one who will oversee its opening.

Mossback's argument for opening the tomb is entirely scholarly, and he made it publicly: the method is as safe as the village's best minds can make it, and the knowledge inside is too valuable to leave buried. He floated the possibility that the tomb could explain the Sacred Spring, the annual sacrifice demanded of Sky-Bough, and even the missing children — though he offered no reason to think so beyond hope.

He also allowed, in the same breath as the village's interests, that the discovery would do no harm to his own standing in the scholarly community.

### The Four on the Bridge

The closers who ambushed the party on the ropeway. None of them carried a weapon; they intended a beating, not a killing.

- **An elderly Katari**, their spokesman. Burly and well past his prime, with the look of a man who had been in many fights and lost few of them. He had lost his daughter two months ago and was certain Old Man Willy was responsible. Killed by Aangry Mank.
- **A scarred Ribbet**, barely three and a half feet tall, with a nasty scar across his left eye and more down his right side. Leapt the length of the bridge onto Zella and slapped her about before Nyx entangled him. Killed by Zella Ironstone.
- **A Kenku bruiser**, big and built for brawling. Saw the blood coming off Zella's longsword, threw himself off the bridge, and flew away. Still alive, and still talking, somewhere in the village.
- **A middle-aged Kenku woman**, the last standing. Surrendered among the bodies and was left alive with them. She knows exactly who killed her friends and why.

## The Openers and the Closers

The village named these factions for itself over the course of a single morning, and they now cut across every other division in Sky-Bough.

**The openers** want the tomb opened. Most of them want it for its own sake: the history of their own forest, the chance of recovering something of the old world while the village is desperate for answers. They were notably unmoved by the argument that there might be treasure inside — several of them took visible offence at it.

**The closers** want the tomb left sealed. They range from the genuinely frightened and the devout, through older villagers who simply hold that old tombs ought not to be disturbed, to a smaller core who will act to prevent it. That core now treats Frayne Castel as its figurehead, on the strength of one angry speech from a balcony, and it was members of that core who met the party on the bridge.

Two points worth remembering:

- Money is still a relatively new idea in Ilyndor, which is part of why Zella's blunt appeal to it landed so strangely. Wealth as a motive is spreading here, but it is not yet the given it is elsewhere in the world.
- The hardliners left the platform promising consequences, hours before any of them laid hands on the party. Whatever else they intend, the bridge was not the whole of it.

## The Half-Moon Stone

A smooth stone, magical, bearing **two half-moons facing each other** — no line drawn between them, and no dots. Old Man Willy picked it up near the outlying farm where the tomb entrance was uncovered, where the researchers had handled it and dropped it again without interest.

Willy values it for its smoothness, which he grades a 9.5 out of 10: whoever carved the mark into it spoiled an otherwise perfect stone. He does not appear to attach any other significance to it, and he kept it when the party left.

What the party established about it:

- It is genuinely magical, as are a select few other stones in the collection — but most of the collection is not.
- No other stone Willy owns bears the same mark. The others carry a world tree, a squiggled star, a wave, and assorted other designs.
- Tasting it brings on a pressure at the edges of the mind — not a presence, a *pressure* — which seems to be trying to get in, and which instinct resists.
- It carries a vision of the tomb.

## Willy's House

Old Man Willy is a Simiah of advanced years — he implied two hundred, in the course of explaining that the party's fingers were too young to handle his best stones. He goes out in a cloak of Kenku feathers, which he gathers from the ground because he cannot stand to see beautiful things wasted, and which gives him the appearance of a moulting old Kenku at a distance.

He is startlingly quick with his hands. He lifted a quill from Finnegan mid-sentence and did not acknowledge having done it.

His house holds a great many objects, between a third and a half of them magical. Nyx identified necrotic auras coming off some of the jars, radiant and holy energy from certain bottles, and primordial energy from other things entirely. It is, in short, exactly the house the village has decided it is — which is why the rumours about him have been so easy to believe.

On the subject of the children, he will neither confirm nor deny. Asked directly, he said he would not tell, because then it would not be a surprise, and because the children have to learn their lesson. He was referring, as far as anyone can tell, to children chiselling his smooth stones.

## The Two Visions

Both point at the same place, and both came unbidden.

**Zella's**, from tasting the stone: two great stone doors in a muddy field beneath a tree, carved with the same marks as the stone. The doors stand open. A trail of blood leads in, and small gold coins lie scattered along it.

**Aangry's**, from tasting the dead man's herbs: a childhood oak that darkened and became a tree of this forest; then the ground beneath it, and the door. What came with it was not fear but *longing* — a sense of something in him left unfulfilled for no reason other than that he has never been there.

The doors the party expect to find at the farm are sealed. In Zella's vision they are open.

## The Dead Man's Daughter

The drawing recovered from the Katari's hidden pocket was made by his daughter: a mother cat, a father cat, and a kitten. She disappeared two months ago.

His account, as his surviving companion gave it: he saw Old Man Willy slip back into his house that same night, carrying a small bundle that was moving. He reported it. Nobody listened. The house was searched and he was told there was nothing there. He went to his death certain of what he had seen.

This makes him one of the few people in Sky-Bough with a specific, personal, first-hand claim against Willy, rather than general suspicion — and he is no longer able to be questioned about it.

## Where Things Stand

- It is late morning. The tomb opens at noon.
- The party is on the forest floor, approaching the farm, with the farm in sight.
- They do not have the half-moon stone; Willy kept it.
- They carry two gold coins, a pouch of crushed herbs, and the drawing.
- Aangry is under the effects of the herbs.
- Two villagers are dead on a rope bridge in the upper boroughs, with two witnesses who know who did it.
- Old Man Willy has been invited along, and is thinking about it.
