# Dragons

Dragons are among the oldest beings in the known world — ancient guardians of cosmic balance who bear an immense inherited legacy of both glory and failure. Once tasked with maintaining equilibrium between the Angels and Devils of the Celestial Age, they failed at that purpose catastrophically. In the long aftermath, many turned to guiding and protecting the emerging mortal races, becoming stewards and teachers during the earliest ages of civilization. Then they withdrew again. In the modern era, they have returned.

---

## Origins and Cosmic Role

Before the rise of mortal civilization, three immortal powers shaped existence: Angels, Devils, and Dragons. The Dragons alone were meant to fully understand the cosmic balance between the creative principle of the Angels and the transformative principle of the Devils, and to maintain equilibrium between them.

For untold ages, the balance held. Then, as the Celestial Age drew toward its catastrophic end, many Dragons grew increasingly detached from their cosmic purpose. Fascinated by material worlds, mortal civilizations, beauty, wealth, and individual ambition, they drifted from their role as arbiters. As Dragonkind withdrew from its responsibilities, the balance between Angels and Devils began to fracture — contributing to the conditions that made the Celestial War inevitable.

This failure became known as **The Silence of Dragons** — a phrase that survived long after the Celestial War and is still used in the modern era to describe catastrophic inaction in the face of growing disaster. The figure of **Aurymathrax — The Worldscale**, foremost Dragon arbiter of the final age before the war, represents this failure: he repeatedly refused decisive action on the grounds that direct intervention would itself destroy balance, and watched as the worlds collapsed around him.

Modern Dragon culture still carries deep inherited shame tied to this era.

---

## The Age of Stewardship

Following the Shattering, the Dragons that remained were burdened by guilt. They took responsibility for guiding the displaced souls and emerging peoples of the surviving world, becoming stewards, teachers, mediators, and guardians during the earliest ages of mortal civilization.

In [[Eldressa]], the Dragons served as the most direct custodians of emerging civilization. They taught agriculture and craftsmanship, mediated disputes between peoples, and helped establish the foundations of what became the mortal kingdoms of the First Age. Many of the earliest Elven kingdoms, Dwarven holds, and Human settlements trace portions of their mythology to ancient Dragon mentors.

Not all Dragons embraced this responsibility. Some withdrew into isolation. Others became consumed by greed, territorial ambition, or obsession with preserving relics of the old world. The infamous **Vorthalyss — The Gilded Tyrant**, whose obsession with wealth and material splendor became legendary even among Dragonkind, is held up as a symbol of the failure to commit to the stewardship role.

**Seredain the White — The Last Dragon Steward** is remembered as one of the final Dragons to openly guide mortal civilization. Seredain served as mediator between early kingdoms for centuries and was widely revered across Eldressa. Their disappearance marked the symbolic end of the Age of Dragon Stewardship. Ancient statues depicting a great white Dragon remain standing in several ruined cities.

The **Accord of Sky and Stone** — a mythic treaty between early Dwarven clans and the Dragon steward **Ithrymath** — exemplifies the role Dragons played in shaping the earliest mortal political arrangements.

---

## The Long Absence

After the Age of Dragon Stewardship, Dragons largely withdrew from mortal affairs. The exact reasons for this withdrawal and where they went remain one of the great unanswered questions of history. The period is documented under "Areas Requiring Further Development" in most scholarly traditions.

What is known is that for centuries, Dragons were largely absent from the known world. They became the subject of legend rather than current events — their deeds remembered in stone and story, their living presence no longer a reality most mortals could expect to encounter.

---

## The Return

Near the end of the Reconstruction Era, the Dragons began to return.

Unlike the Dragon Stewards of ancient history, the returning Dragons did not seek to govern the mortal races. Instead they acted as ambassadors, explorers, teachers, and protectors — acknowledging the maturity that the mortal civilizations had developed in their absence.

For the first time in recorded history, Dragons regularly traveled between continents. **Dragon Riders** emerged as messengers and diplomats capable of crossing oceans and mountain ranges in days — transforming communication across a world that had previously relied on slow sea routes. Information that once took months or years to travel could suddenly spread across the world with unprecedented speed.

The returning Dragons also played a direct role in the founding of [[Valtiron]], helping broker the creation of the neutral political ground where [[Eldressa]]'s fractured nations could meet and negotiate.

Dragon Rider embassies are now established in [[Meridia]], and Dragons serve as the primary communication and diplomatic network connecting the continents.

---

## Present Day

In 300 AU, Dragons are an active presence in the modern world for the first time in centuries. Their return during the Age of Reconnection transformed global politics and communication, and their continued involvement in diplomacy and travel shapes the modern era in ways that are still being understood.

Dragon culture remains private and difficult for outsiders to fully comprehend. The shame of the Silence of Dragons is deeply embedded in Draconic tradition, and most Dragons who engage with the mortal world do so with an awareness of both their ancient power and their historic failure to use it wisely.

The Dragon Rider network remains the fastest and most reliable means of long-distance communication and travel in the known world.
