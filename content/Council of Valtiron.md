# Council of Valtiron

The Council of Valtiron is the closest institution the known world possesses to an international governing body. It convenes in [[Valtiron]] — the neutral city built in the crater of the destroyed Great Mountain — and brings together representatives from the major powers of [[Eldressa]] and beyond to negotiate treaties, settle disputes, coordinate trade, and address matters affecting the wider world.

It possesses significant diplomatic influence. It does not possess a standing army, the authority to impose binding decisions, or the power to compel any sovereign state. Its effectiveness depends entirely on the cooperation and good faith of its participants.

---

## Structure and Function

The Council functions as a mixture of diplomatic forum and cooperative assembly — closer in character to a combination of the great international bodies of history than to a single model of governance. Representatives arrive from across Eldressa and, increasingly, from the other continents as well. They debate, negotiate, and reach agreements that carry the weight of collective endorsement rather than legal enforcement.

Decisions reached within the Council chambers carry enormous political weight despite this limitation. To defy a consensus of the Council is to defy the collected diplomatic opinion of most of the major powers of the world simultaneously — a position few rulers find comfortable.

The **Chancellor of Valtiron** serves as the presiding officer of the Council, managing its proceedings, mediating between competing interests, and representing the institution to the wider world. The Chancellor is not a ruler — they govern little territory of their own beyond [[Valtiron]] and the nearby settlement of **Unvis** — but the position is one of the most influential in the world.

---

## History

The Council was established during the **Age of Reconnection**, when the returning [[Dragons]] helped broker the creation of [[Valtiron]] as a neutral political ground. The choice to build the city within the crater of the destroyed Great Mountain was deliberate — the site of one of the greatest catastrophes of the Demon Wars transformed into a place dedicated to preventing future catastrophe.

In its early decades, the Council focused primarily on Eldressan affairs: managing the reconstruction after the Second Demon War, negotiating borders between the recovering kingdoms, and establishing the political norms that would define the continent going forward.

As the Age of Reconnection connected the continents more fully, the Council's remit expanded. Representatives from [[Velkrand]], [[Meridia]], and [[Ilyndor]] began attending with increasing regularity. By the Age of Discovery, it had effectively become an institution of global significance despite remaining technically an Eldressan body.

---

## Current Leadership

**Chancellor Serazeth Vael (Demon, Female)** is the current presiding Chancellor — a direct descendant of a figure from the Unification peace accords and the first [[Demons|Demon]] to hold the position. She is widely regarded as one of the most skilled diplomats alive.

Her appointment was not without controversy. Many welcomed it as a demonstration of how far Demon integration had progressed in the centuries since the Unification. Others viewed it as politically premature. Chancellor Vael has responded to this controversy primarily by being exceptionally good at her job.

## Major Members

The following powers maintain permanent representation on the Council:

**Eldressa:**
- [[Valtiron]] (host city)
- Kingdom of Brethil (Queen Aeloria Starbloom)
- Dwarven Holds (High Chieftain Brokk Stoneheart)
- Kingdom of Algade (King Edric Valen)
- [[Rivara]] (Magistrate Helena Fairharbor)
- Coral (First Speaker Neris Tidecaller)
- Yotl (Speaker Tovan Reedwalker)

**Other regions:**
- [[Velkrand]] Compact (Twin Thrones of Grimwald)
- [[Meridia]] (Governess Admiral Celeste Fairwind)
- Ilyndor observers (informal)

## Limitations

The Council's greatest strength — its inclusivity and voluntary membership — is also its greatest weakness. Any member can withdraw. Any agreement can be repudiated by a future government. Enforcement of Council resolutions depends on the willingness of member states to act together, which is never guaranteed.

Critics argue the Council is a forum for the powerful to legitimize decisions they have already made privately. Supporters argue that even imperfect international cooperation is vastly preferable to the alternative.

Both are, to some degree, correct.
