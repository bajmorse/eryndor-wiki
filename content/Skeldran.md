# Skeldran

![[skeldran.png|900]]

## Location

Skeldran is a shattered continent, the oldest known ruin of the First Age. It lies surrounded by the Obsidian Reaches — waters famous for dense fog, obsidian reefs, violent currents, and unpredictable storms that make navigation among the most dangerous in the world. Black obsidian cliffs rise from the sea along much of its coastline. The continent is accessible from [[Meridian Isles|Meridia]] and [[Hrothan]] by determined sailors, but no regular trade routes pass through it and no major power claims it as a territory.

---

## Description

Skeldran feels ancient in a way that even [[Eldressa]]'s oldest ruins do not. The catastrophe that broke it occurred long before the first mortal kingdoms rose elsewhere — before the First Demon War, before the Age of Dragon Stewardship, before most of what passes for recorded history. The continent has been shattered for so long that the shattered state feels natural.

Fossilized Leviathan remains dominate the landscape. Bones the size of city blocks protrude from cliffsides and beaches. Entire mountain ridges are formed from the spines of creatures that died so long ago their names survive only in fragments. In some settlements, people live within the ribcages and vertebrae of beings that were extinct before their ancestors were born.

Dense fog is a near-constant presence. The seas never fully recovered. Storms appear without warning. Ships vanish.

Most people who go to Skeldran are looking for something. Most of them are disappointed. Some of them do not return.

## Geography

**The fractured landmass** is the defining feature of Skeldran. What was once a single continent now consists of numerous islands, peninsulas, and landmasses separated by flooded valleys, collapsed mountain ranges, and channels carved by the violence of the Leviathan War. Navigation between landmasses requires constant care. Maps of Skeldran are notoriously unreliable, as sections of coastline continue to shift, collapse, or emerge unpredictably.

**The Obsidian Reaches** surround the continent — waters so dangerous that most sailors give them a wide berth. The fog is dense and directional landmarks are scarce. Hidden shoals, obsidian reefs, submerged ruins, and the skeletal remains of Leviathans beneath the waves make precise navigation nearly impossible without local knowledge. Storms can appear within minutes and entire vessels occasionally vanish without explanation.

**The Dragon Waters** — the common name for the most treacherous sections of the surrounding seas — are littered with the remains of ships that attempted the crossing without adequate preparation or guidance.

**Fossilized Leviathan remains** are found throughout the continent. Some are partially buried, with only fins, spines, or sections of skull visible above the ground. Others lie exposed across entire ridgelines. A few have been incorporated into geography itself — rivers flowing through ribcages, settlements built within skull cavities, roads following the curve of ancient vertebral columns.

**Greywatch** is the primary inhabited settlement and the only significant permanent community in Skeldran. Built at one of the continent's more navigable anchorages, it serves as a staging point for expeditions into the interior and a trading post for the salvaged materials and relics that constitute most of Skeldran's exports.

**Drowned valleys and black fjords** fill much of the interior, remnants of what were once mountain ranges and lowland plains before the Sundering Tide reshaped everything.

## Races / Biology

Skeldran's ancient peoples were three: the **Selkari**, the **Tidemarked**, and the **Corvani**. Each civilization flourished during the Age of Three Crowns under the protection of their respective Leviathan patrons. The Selkari were the most maritime of the three, the Tidemarked builders of the great coastal cities, and the Corvani inhabitants of the high cliffs and storm-raked northern reaches.

All three civilizations collapsed with the Leviathans. Their descendants either scattered across the world during the aftermath of the Leviathan War or vanished entirely. Stories persist of hidden Selkari enclaves, isolated Corvani descendants, and forgotten communities living among the Leviathan bones, but no credible evidence has ever confirmed their existence.

The modern permanent population of Skeldran is minimal. Those who remain tend to be:

- **Explorers and archaeologists** pursuing the relics and ruins of the ancient civilizations
- **Treasure hunters** seeking the drowned city of Vaeloris and other lost sites
- **Fishermen** who work the dangerous coastal waters for species found nowhere else
- **Scholars** studying the Leviathans and the geological history of the Sundering
- **Scavengers** who harvest Leviathan bone, blackstone, and salvaged materials
- **Those seeking isolation**, for whom Skeldran's inaccessibility is a feature rather than a problem

The continent's wildlife is sparse on land but significant in the surrounding waters. Several large marine predators inhabit the Dragon Waters, some of which are old enough that sailors are not entirely certain whether they are natural creatures or remnants of the Leviathan age. The deep channels between shattered landmasses shelter ecosystems that have evolved in near-complete isolation for thousands of years.

## History

Skeldran's history divides neatly into two eras: before the Leviathan War, and after it.

**The Age of Three Crowns** was the golden age. The Selkari, Tidemarked, and Corvani built civilizations of genuine sophistication beneath the protection of their Leviathan patrons — the nine great beings divided into three Triunes, each associated with one people. Trade flourished between the civilizations. Great cities rose. The seas were navigable, the skies clear. Ancient records describe Skeldran as one of the most prosperous regions of the early world.

The decline began with the **Dispute of Three Horizons** — not a single conflict but a gradual accumulation of territorial disputes, competing claims over fishing grounds and migration routes and sacred sites, and failed diplomatic attempts to resolve them. The rivalries between peoples eventually spread upward to their Leviathan patrons.

The **Breaking of Nyth Reach** marked the first direct battle between Leviathans, when Korvax the Thunderwing and Tharos the Breakwater clashed near the coast and shattered entire sections of shoreline. It is generally treated as the true beginning of the Leviathan War.

The war escalated in scale and catastrophe. The **Drowning of Vaeloris** — the greatest city of the Tidemarked — occurred when three Leviathans clashed in its harbor and the resulting tidal waves sank much of the city. The **Burning of Stormspire**, the great Corvani cliff-city, was destroyed when a falling Leviathan body crashed into it during battle. The continent began breaking apart under the physical force of the conflict.

The **Sundering Tide** was the final phase — the moment when the surviving Leviathans unleashed devastation on a scale that reshaped the continent itself. Entire regions sank. Mountain ranges collapsed. The sea swallowed great sections of land. By the war's end, all nine Leviathans had perished and the once-unified continent was the shattered ruin known today.

The **Silence of the Leviathans** — the day the final Leviathan died — marks the end of the First Age of Skeldran. The surviving peoples scattered. Their civilizations collapsed. The age of Leviathan stewardship was over forever.

## Present Day

Skeldran in 300 AU is widely considered uninhabited.

No recognized government exists. The continent is not claimed by any major power. Expeditions into its interior typically originate from [[Meridian Isles|Meridia]] or [[Hrothan]] and are usually funded by private interests rather than any state.

**Greywatch** is the only permanent settlement of any significance, and it is less a city than a fortified outpost — civilization surviving rather than thriving. Its population consists primarily of those with reasons to be in Skeldran specifically: traders in salvaged goods, expedition outfitters, fishermen, and a small scholarly community maintaining the only functioning archive of research on the continent's ancient peoples.

The **Northern Star Trading Company** is the principal organization maintaining regular contact with Skeldran. It operates routes transporting blackstone, Leviathan bone, ancient artifacts, fossil materials, and salvaged relics to markets in [[Meridian Isles|Meridia]] and beyond. Its captains are among the most experienced sailors in the Dragon Waters.

The question of whether any descendants of the Selkari, Tidemarked, or Corvani remain somewhere in Skeldran's unexplored interior is one that scholars and explorers periodically revisit. The continent is large, its geography difficult, and most of it has never been systematically surveyed. The most honest answer is: no one knows.

What is known is that the continent retains one of the most complete records of the Leviathans in existence. For anyone willing to survive the crossing and endure the conditions, Skeldran holds more of the ancient world's secrets than anywhere else currently accessible.
