# Hrothan

![[hrothan.png|900]]

## Location

Hrothan is the frozen southern continent — and in a world where south means cold, it is the coldest inhabited land in existence. Vast tundra expanses, glaciers, frozen rivers, and ice shelves define most of its surface. The continent is anchored around the Pale Expanse, a great open tundra region surrounding the massive frozen inland lake known as **Lake Varglas**, or the Great Frozen Lake. Mountain ranges — the Icebite Range and the Howlspine Mountains — rise in the north and east, providing the only significant elevation on an otherwise flat and featureless horizon. Cliffwarden's Wood occupies a coastal stretch of the continent's northern edges.

---

## Description

Hrothan does not welcome visitors. It does not welcome its own inhabitants, either — it simply tolerates those strong enough to survive it.

This is the homeland of humanity, and humanity left. The migration northward into [[Eldressa]] was not an adventure undertaken for curiosity. It was a response to conditions that no longer supported life at the scale the continent once sustained. The peoples who remain — nomadic human tribes, Goliath clans, Giant lineages — are not the ones who failed to leave. They are the ones who chose the old world over the new one, or who simply belong to a different relationship with cold and hardship than most of the rest of Eryndor can imagine.

Hrothan should not be understood as ruined or post-apocalyptic. It is primordial. The land is old in a way that resists the word "ancient," because ancient implies that something happened there and stopped. Nothing stopped in Hrothan. The glaciers still move. The Colossi may still sleep beneath the ice. The storms still come.

## Geography

**The Pale Expanse** is the defining feature of Hrothan — an enormous open tundra stretching across the continental interior, dominated by seasonal migrations, sparse vegetation, frozen rivers, and the occasional isolated cairn or standing stone left by travelers who passed through centuries ago.

**Lake Varglas** — the Great Frozen Lake — is the largest freshwater lake in Hrothan and one of the most revered locations in the north. For most of the year it appears as an endless expanse of ice stretching beyond the horizon. Many northern traditions claim it was formed when a dying Colossus lay down upon the tundra and slowly sank into the earth. The lake is both a trade route — entire clans cross its frozen surface during winter migrations — and a sacred place. According to legend, on particularly clear nights the ice becomes transparent enough to reveal visions of the past beneath its surface.

Many stories also speak of something enormous sleeping beneath the lake. Descriptions vary — ancient Colossus, lost Leviathan, something without a name — but most northerners agree that whatever lies there should be left alone. Tremors periodically ripple across the lake without explanation.

**The Icebite Range and Howlspine Mountains** rise in the north and east, home to the Goliath clans and the territory where mountains provide some shelter from the open tundra. The eastern mountains also contain the geothermal activity that sustains the **Steamvein Settlement** — the only permanent settlement of any size on the continent.

**Cliffwarden's Wood** occupies the coastal north — one of the few forested regions in Hrothan, now subject to commercial forestry operations backed by Meridian interests. Its expansion has created significant tension with local tribes and nearby Giants.

**Ancient migration routes** cross the continent in several directions. These routes are marked by cairns, standing stones, burial mounds, primitive shrines, and ancient campsites — the accumulated physical record of countless generations of movement across the frozen world.

## Races / Biology

**Humans** originated in Hrothan. Though most of humanity has since migrated northward and built civilizations across [[Eldressa]] and beyond, the northern tribes remain — roaming the Pale Expanse surrounding Lake Varglas, following ancient migration patterns, hunting the remaining megafauna, and maintaining oral traditions that preserve some of the oldest human myths on Eryndor.

**Goliaths** dominate the Icebite Range and Howlspine Mountains. Their culture centers around the mountain clans, with leadership determined through a combination of martial excellence and demonstrated judgment. Goliath communities tend to be insular and self-sufficient, with clan affiliation remaining the primary social identity.

**Giants** inhabit the southeastern portions of the continent, occupying territories that most other peoples avoid by tradition or common sense. Giant lineages are ancient and trace their cultural heritage back to the earliest ages of Hrothan, including figures like **Skara White-Eye**, whose legendary sight reportedly allowed her to foresee the collapse of the Icehorn herds long before it occurred. The current **Speaker of Stones** carries the same name by lineage.

**Icehorns** once dominated the continent as enormous migratory tundra beasts that supported most of Hrothan's human cultures — providing food, leather, bone, shelter materials, and fuel. Their population collapsed during the Long Winter, triggering the famine and conflict that ultimately drove the first great human migration south. Smaller populations persist today, but nothing approaching the great herds of the First Age.

Other megafauna persist in the more remote regions: mammoth-type creatures, large predators adapted to the tundra, and the occasional creature of unclear provenance near the lake.

The **Colossi** — the great primordial stewards of Hrothan's First Age — are believed by many traditions to still exist somewhere beneath the ice, sleeping rather than gone. The periodic unexplained tremors across Lake Varglas are cited as evidence. No living being has confirmed a Colossus sighting in recorded history.

## History

Hrothan's history is the history of survival.

The **Colossi** presided over the continent during the First Age — immense primordial beings composed of living stone, ice, and permafrost, some so vast that early peoples mistook them for landscape features. Unlike the Dragon Stewards of [[Eldressa]] or the Woodland Guardians of [[Ilyndor]], the Colossi did not guide civilization. They embodied the harsh realities of the north itself: endurance, patience, resilience, and survival. Ancient migration routes across Hrothan are said to have been carved by their movements. Many traditions claim they merely sleep now, waiting.

The **Long Winter** was the defining catastrophe of early Hrothan. The collapse of the great Icehorn herds — through disease, disrupted migration, or causes no tradition fully agrees upon — brought famine across the continent. Human tribes competed over scarce resources. Conflicts with Goliath clans and Giants became increasingly common. **Lyra Snowsong**, one of the most beloved figures in human folklore, spent the height of the Long Winter traveling between scattered tribes preserving the oral histories she feared would be lost entirely.

**Kaelor Icewalker**, the most famous figure in human history, spent years mapping safe routes through glaciers and mountain passes before leading his people south across the frozen Frostfire Strait into [[Eldressa]]. Whether he truly existed is debated. Every human culture preserves some version of his story.

The **Gathering of a Thousand Fires** stands as one of the rare moments of unity in Hrothan's history — representatives of Human tribes, Goliath clans, and several Giant lineages gathering on the shores of Lake Varglas, burning fires across the frozen lake for seven nights, settling old feuds, arranging marriages, establishing trade routes, and exchanging songs and stories. Many historians regard it as the moment when Hrothan's peoples briefly saw themselves as fellow survivors of the same world rather than competitors for it.

The **Last Hunt** — a legendary event in which representatives of all three peoples tracked and hunted the final great Icehorn herd together rather than fighting over it — is treated across many traditions as the symbolic close of the old Hrothan and the beginning of whatever came after.

## Present Day

Hrothan in 300 AU is the least centralized continent in the world. No government exists. No overarching authority claims the continent. Power resides with individual tribes, clans, and local leaders, with temporary alliances occasionally forming during crises.

**The Human tribes** roam the Pale Expanse surrounding Lake Varglas, following ancient migration routes and maintaining the oral traditions that have sustained their culture across thousands of years. The **High Storykeeper**, currently **Eirik Snowborn**, is one of the most respected leaders among the northern tribes — a position defined by the stewardship of cultural memory as much as by any political authority.

**The Goliath Clans** dominate the Icebite Range and Howlspine Mountains under the leadership of **Clanfather Torvek Skybreaker**, widely respected among the mountain communities for his combination of physical authority and careful judgment.

**The Giant Territories** of southeastern Hrothan are represented by the **Speaker of Stones**, currently **Skara White-Eye** — a spiritual leader whose name and lineage traces back to the legendary ancient seer. The Giants interact with outside traders and visitors rarely and on their own terms.

**Steamvein Settlement** represents the only significant permanent settlement on the continent — a growing community centered around geothermal springs in the eastern mountains that provide reliable heat in a land where warmth is survival. The Settlement Warden, **Maren Frostbrook**, leads what functions as Hrothan's only genuinely multicultural community, drawing inhabitants from every major group in the north.

The **Cliffwarden Timber Company**, a controversial Meridian-backed forestry operation in Cliffwarden's Wood, represents the most visible point of tension on the modern continent. Its commercial expansion into the coastal forests has created friction with local Human tribes and nearby Giants, raising questions about what outside interests owe to the peoples of the lands they operate in — a question Hrothan has rarely had to ask before.

Southern historians sometimes describe Hrothan as primitive. The peoples of Hrothan generally view the south as soft, fleeting, and disconnected from the truths the world has been teaching since long before anyone thought to write them down.
