# The Mayor

Connected to [[Full Session Notes]] in the player wiki.

Avoid deciding she's secretly corrupt unless something emerging during play pushes that way — a well-intentioned mayor making questionable decisions is considerably richer.

## Current State

The mayor is a young Kenku woman, energetic and ambitious, particularly popular among Sky-Bough's younger inhabitants. She controls the commercial distribution of [[Skybound Pale Ale]] and has been cultivating relationships with merchants, travellers, and political contacts from beyond Ilyndor, quietly prioritizing dwindling supply for them.

## Established GM Truths

- Her name is Rikka Corvane.
- She was elected on a wave of youth support — an unusually contested race where the older establishment split their votes among several candidates, and Rikka rode a surge of younger villagers wanting change into office.
- Younger villagers love her mainly because she represents a break from the old, dogmatic ways they feel Sky-Bough has been stuck in — visible through things like [[Skybound Pale Ale]]'s trade growth, more visitors from abroad, and more trade delegations. Her own personal energy and charisma reinforce that feeling; she's genuinely present and engaging, not just a symbol of change from a distance.
- Her relationship with [[Joe and the Bent Feather|Joe]] is genuinely good, despite public perception that she's taking advantage of him or that he's angling for control over Skybound or the tournament.
- Her outsider relationships aren't personal gain or favoritism — she foresees the Pact/spring crisis worsening and is quietly building alliances and goodwill against a future where the village needs help, while also safeguarding [[Skybound Pale Ale]]'s legacy beyond Sky-Bough. This is exactly what she's keeping quiet — not out of guilt, but because saying "I think we're heading for real trouble" out loud could do more damage than the current resentment already does.
- The foreigners are deliberately younger travelers and merchants from beyond Ilyndor — not a specific faction (not Dragon Riders, not tied to [[The Sea People]]) — which is part of why it visually reads as globalization/favoritism to older villagers.
- The mayor's office inherits the most complete surviving account of [[The Ancient Pact]]'s true history — but that account has been reshaped by generations of mayors to fit their own era's morality, so what she was given is an edited version, not the truth. She doesn't currently know it's incomplete.
- Part of the resentment toward her outsider relationships traces back to last year's escaped sacrifice: an ordinary Sky-Bough villager in their thirties who had fallen in love with a newer resident from Meridia. That outsider begged them not to go through with the sacrifice — and when they escaped, villagers who already distrusted the growing outsider presence pointed to it as proof that letting more foreigners in was eroding the old ways. See [[The Ancient Pact]].
- Older villagers' distrust of her is the flip side of what younger villagers love: to them, her outward-looking changes read as selling off pieces of Sky-Bough's traditions and identity to outsiders rather than honoring the old ways. The ale shortage has sharpened that resentment considerably (see [[Skybound Pale Ale]]), and last year's escaped sacrifice gave it a concrete, recent example to point to.
- She is aware that resentment is building against her, but underestimates just how deep and widespread it's actually become among older villagers.
- Her knowledge of the Sacred Spring is only surface-level — she knows it's receding, but the edited account her office inherited doesn't include the specific truth that the Fae is the actual source of the spring's magic. See [[The Sacred Spring]].
- She's worried about the declining harvest but not certain of its ultimate cause, though like most of Sky-Bough she's connected it to the failed sacrifice. She's actually harboured private doubts for a while that the sacrifice was ever truly doing anything at all — but the coincidental timing of the harvest failing, rising natural aggression, and the Sacred Spring draining together is too much to dismiss as unrelated.
- The Parkers reported the harvest's gradient pattern to her directly — worst near the forest, tapering off with distance (see [[The Parkers and the Harvest]]) — which is far more pointed evidence than mere coincidental timing. She now has real reason to suspect the trouble radiates outward from the ritual site itself, even though she still has no idea what's actually leaking from it or why.
- When last year's sacrifice escaped, she made the pragmatic call not to chase them down — the damage to the ritual was already done by that point, and pursuit wouldn't have undone it. Publicly this reads as cold practicality, but privately it was as much sympathy as pragmatism. Some part of her was even secretly glad the ritual failed — it forces Sky-Bough, out of necessity, toward confronting its reliance on the Pact, which lines up with her own long-held hope of ending that reliance.
- As mayor, she must preside over the sacrifices — it isn't something she's been able to avoid. The general populace does not witness the ritual itself; only those required to administer it are present. See [[The Ancient Pact]].
- She carries a serious internal moral conflict about the Ancient Pact — she does not like the idea of sacrificing a villager every year — but shares the same resigned acceptance as the rest of Sky-Bough, currently believing in its necessity. That resignation isn't passive, though: she actively wants to find a way to end the village's reliance on the ritual for good, tying into her broader hope that Sky-Bough can someday survive without it.
- Despite what some villagers think, she genuinely is proud of Sky-Bough's past and its traditions — she isn't trying to erase them. What she wants, above all, is for the village to prosper: to become a real trade hub and a beacon of Ilyndoran civilization in the south, keeping pace with a changing world rather than being left behind by it. She also wants Sky-Bough to be able to survive without the Pact someday — but that desire predates and is unrelated to last year's failed sacrifice and the current crisis; it's a long-held ambition, not a reaction to recent events.
