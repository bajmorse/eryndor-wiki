# Skybound Pale Ale

Connected to [[Full Session Notes]] in the player wiki.

A fantastic mundane thread — something as trivial as beer becomes the point where economics, politics, tradition, and supernatural problems intersect. [[The Sacred Spring]]'s water is an essential ingredient.

## Current State

Joe brews Skybound Pale Ale using a small amount of water from the Sacred Spring — the ingredient that gives it its distinctive character and that can't be exported. The village mayor controls its commercial distribution while Joe controls its production. With the spring drying up, Joe can brew far less than before, and the mayor has been prioritizing what remains for merchants, travellers, and political contacts from beyond Ilyndor.

## Established GM Truths

- Joe is not deliberately withholding or reducing production for personal advantage — the shortage is entirely a consequence of the Sacred Spring drying up. See [[Joe and the Bent Feather]].
- Joe does not care about Skybound's profits for himself; most of it funds [[The Sky-Bough Martial Arts Tournament]], and the remainder he donates back to the town.
- Villagers blame Joe rather than the mayor partly through simple proximity (he physically makes it), and partly because some pro-mayor villagers have wrongly concluded his indifference to profit masks a scheme to seize greater economic control. No one meaningfully benefits from this belief — it's a misunderstanding, not an engineered campaign.
- Skybound's distinctive character and slight glow come from the same source as the spring's anti-aging property — the water's proximity to the bound Fae's garden-work. See [[The Sacred Spring]].
- Joe knows the ale carries the spring's supernatural properties — it's common knowledge among villagers generally, not a secret. Nobody actually understands the deeper mechanism behind it, Joe included — see [[The Sacred Spring]].
- Skybound genuinely carries a diffused trace of the spring's magic through the brewing process. It doesn't extend the drinker's life, but it does make them feel rejuvenated — a real, non-placebo part of why the ale is so beloved, not just its flavor.
- Only Joe knows the recipe.
- The Joe/mayor production-and-trade split was never formally negotiated — it settled into place informally. The mayor was a regular, genuine customer at the Bent Feather who loved the ale and wanted it enjoyed by more people.
- Only Joe's share of Skybound's profits funds the tournament; the town's share (via the mayor) funds other things. The mayor does not skim off the top, despite being accused of it — though the party has no way to know this for a fact yet.
- Skybound is an important piece of Sky-Bough's economy, but not its only significant trade good.
- The current shortage is noticeable — less ale, rising worry — but hasn't reached open anger or the tavern turning locals away entirely.
- That the spring is the reason for the decline is effectively an open secret: most people know the ale uses spring water (without knowing exactly how), and everyone knows the spring is drying up. No one's officially connected the dots aloud, but anyone paying attention can.
- The mayor's outsider relationships are not personal favoritism or currying favor for herself. She sees the Pact and Sacred Spring failing and foresees real hardship ahead for the village — so she's deliberately building goodwill with foreigners and travelers (people who read as "new/young" to older villagers, which is exactly why it looks like favoritism), hoping for allies who'd come to the village's aid, and to make sure Skybound Pale Ale's fame endures even if the spring ever stops production entirely.

## Rumours / What NPCs Believe

Villagers see there's no ale for them anymore while the mayor's foreign friends never seem thirsty. Because Joe physically makes the ale, much of the anger has landed on him — including the rumour that he's somehow trying to "maintain control over the town" through it.
