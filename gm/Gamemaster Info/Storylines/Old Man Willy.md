# Old Man Willy

Connected to [[Full Session Notes]] in the player wiki.

Canonically important: Old Man Willy did nothing wrong. Therefore the question isn't whether he's responsible — it's why everything makes it look like he is. Play him weird as hell; don't undermine the players' suspicions by making him obviously sweet and innocent. He did nothing wrong. He should nevertheless behave exactly like a man who has done several things wrong.

## Current State

Willy is an elderly Simiah who has spent decades collecting strange objects from throughout the forest — old coins, broken idols, bits of pottery, weapons, bones, carved stones, and fragments of things nobody recognizes. He's recently started appearing at the Bent Feather every evening, despite having avoided the tavern for years. He's recognized the symbol appearing where the children disappear, because he owns something bearing the same symbol, and he knows where he found it — he's been coming to the tavern because he wants to tell somebody, and just hasn't decided who he trusts.

## Established GM Truths

- Willy recognizes the symbol associated with the disappearances — he owns something bearing it, and knows where he found it.
- He's started visiting the Bent Feather nightly because he wants to tell someone what he knows, but hasn't decided who he trusts — and on top of that, he's simply never been good at approaching or talking to people, which has made even starting the conversation harder than the decision itself.
- What changed and broke his routine was recognizing the symbol and realizing what it might mean.
- The object Willy owns bearing the symbol is an ancient ceremonial carved stone, made by an extinct culture to commemorate a chosen host of the Mycelium Host — found by him, not traded, somewhere deep in the forest years ago, with no idea of its true age or origin. See [[The Missing Children]]. It's the strangest thing he owns, precisely because nothing about its make or material has ever matched anything else he's found. He has no understanding of what it actually depicts or means — only that it bears the exact same mark now turning up at each disappearance, which is what convinced him something is deeply wrong.
- There is no deeper meaning behind why Willy collects relics — no scholarly pursuit, no search for something specific, no unresolved personal question driving it. He simply finds genuine joy in collecting them, and that's the whole truth of it. An outside observer could read it as a substitute for companionship or a byproduct of a lonely life, but that reading says more about the observer's assumptions than about Willy — it isn't what's actually true.
- Willy isn't a relic hunter in any active sense — he never goes out seeking relics, and he essentially never trades for them, since trading would imply some specific quality or outcome he's after, which he isn't. He simply enjoys wandering, mostly through the deep ancient woodland around Sky-Bough but also out to the cliffs near the Summering Sea and the mountains to the southeast, sometimes cave-diving in any of the three. He brings things back because he happens to see them and enjoys collecting them, not because he was looking for them.
- The "necromancer" reputation comes from an accumulation of surface-level, misread details, not any single event: he collects bones among his other relics — sometimes unusual ones found in caves or bearing strange engravings, exactly the kind of interesting find he can't resist bringing home — combined with living alone, his general oddness, and stories like the bones once spotted outside his house. Whether those particular bones were mundane or something stranger is deliberately left open. Willy himself has no idea how any of this looks to outsiders and has never given his own reputation a moment's thought. He is not a necromancer, and has never practiced anything resembling necromancy.
- Willy has never encouraged the rumours, nor made any effort to quell them — both would require interacting with far more people than he's comfortable with. He is aware the rumours exist, but is genuinely oblivious that his own habits and behavior are actually what's causing them.
- Willy knows nothing about the Sacred Spring beyond common knowledge — he actively avoids the area, since it draws more visitors than the rest of the forest and he'd rather not risk running into people there.
- Willy has no real knowledge of the ancient tomb and has never been to it — it was only recently unearthed. Villagers connecting him to it are simply drawing the wrong conclusion from the skeletons found inside (see [[The Forgotten Hero and the Tomb]]). He does, coincidentally, own a bone he picked up near that area at some point, before the tomb was uncovered, purely because it had interesting dark patterns on it — a detail known to nobody, not even the party, and one Willy himself has never connected to the tomb at all.
- The rumours about Willy have no single originator — it's organic, town-wide gossip, the same way rumours spread elsewhere in Sky-Bough, with no one person driving or benefiting from it.
