# The Forgotten Hero and the Tomb

Connected to [[Full Session Notes]] in the player wiki.

## Current State

Generations ago, something emerged from beneath the forest — stories call it a demon. A travelling sorcerer confronted it but couldn't destroy it, instead sealing the creature underground and promising to return someday to finish what they began. They never returned, and the story faded into folklore. Farmers clearing new land have now uncovered the entrance to the tomb; the seals and inscriptions are real, and something is definitely inside. Local priests intend to open it, "for research purposes."

## Established GM Truths

- This predates the Age of Reconnection and the return of the Dragons — there were no Dragon Riders yet when this happened.
- The "demon" was real. The sealing has no connection to [[The Ancient Pact]] — different era, different power, unrelated binding.
- The "travelling sorcerer" was a wandering Faun sorceress, not native to Sky-Bough, who came upon the village while it was suffering under the demon's presence and chose to help. She badly overestimated her own abilities and was genuinely in over her head against something far stronger than she'd faced before.
- Unwilling to admit she couldn't actually destroy it, she sealed the demon as best her real (limited) power allowed — an imperfect, improvised binding rather than the clean, masterful working the legend remembers. She promised the village she'd return to finish the job, but she had no real intention of ever coming back; the promise was about saving face, not a genuine commitment.
- What she actually sealed in the tomb was only a vessel — the demon's true essence had already latched onto her during the confrontation, unnoticed. She left Sky-Bough believing she'd won, carrying the demon with her the whole time, and it slowly corrupted her into something darker over the years that followed.
- She is still out there today, somewhere in the world, having long since been consumed by what latched onto her.
- This all happened well before the Unification — long enough ago that humans and elves hadn't yet arrived in these lands, which is part of why she was a Faun rather than a half-elf. Whatever has kept her going since is bound up with the corruption itself, not a natural lifespan.
- There was already a small village on this ground when the demon was sealed, long predating modern Sky-Bough. The tomb was simply forgotten over the generations that followed — the physical entrance eroded and became overgrown, and the story faded into vague folklore the ordinary way, with nothing malicious keeping it hidden.
- The tomb's exposure now is pure coincidence: farmers, hoping to find more fertile ground amid the failing harvest, were clearing land they'd never touched before and uncovered the long-overgrown, root-choked entrance. Nothing deliberately revealed it.
- What's actually sealed inside is a small residual amount of the demon's essence — left behind in the vessel even after the bulk of it latched onto the sorceress — manifesting as a creature roughly equivalent to a Tier 1 "Lesser Demon" statblock. Real danger, but nowhere near the scale of the original legend.
- The tomb also holds a skeleton or two, unrelated to the demon itself, which have already fed local rumours and false accusations pointed at [[Old Man Willy]].
- The seal was never built to last generations — it was only ever meant to buy the sorceress enough time to flee town before anything tested it. It has held only because nobody has seriously tried to break it since. It's genuinely fragile, and would come apart fairly easily under any real attempt to open it.
- The priests' interest is genuine scholarly curiosity, nothing more calculated — the inscriptions and seals are authentically ancient and real, and that authenticity alone is what convinced them it's worth studying, especially with the village hungry for any answers about the old world right now. The legend of the sorcerer has been forgotten so completely that nobody opposing them has any specific reason tied to the tomb's actual history — objectors simply hold the general instinct that old tombs like this should be left undisturbed.
- Her real name was Iris Vaneet; she performed and traveled under the sorceress name **Lady Faewrought, Vesper of the Green Dawn**.
