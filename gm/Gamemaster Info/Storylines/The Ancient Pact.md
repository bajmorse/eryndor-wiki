# The Ancient Pact

Connected to [[Full Session Notes]] in the player wiki.

The most foundational mystery in Sky-Bough — its answers potentially inform several other threads.

## Current State

Once each year, before the growing season, Sky-Bough selects one resident by lot and sacrifices them. In exchange, the surrounding forest stays relatively safe, dangerous creatures keep their distance, and the harvest holds. Last year, for the first time in living memory, the selected sacrifice escaped before the ritual could be completed, leaving the pact unfulfilled. Months later, crop yields are down, wildlife is behaving strangely, predators are appearing closer to settled areas, and the Sacred Spring is receding.

## Established GM Truths

- The pact was struck with a Fae creature lured out of the Faewilds/Shadowfell long ago and bound at what is now the Sacred Spring's source. See [[The Sacred Spring]].
- The sacrifice mechanic is a mix of two things: it's a literal life-for-life bargain from the original binding (equivalent exchange, keyed to "like for like"), and the sentient death releases the specific soul-stuff the binding wards run on — the sacrifice doesn't feed the Fae, it renews the cage. Sentient sacrifice was always the pact's term, never a later corruption of something gentler.
- Both of Sky-Bough's boons — the good harvest and the forest staying safe (predators and dangers keeping their distance) — come from this same source, not two separate arrangements.
- Last year's failed sacrifice is directly and solely responsible for the souring harvest and the receding Sacred Spring: the wards are weakening for the first time in living memory.
- The Fae itself is not malevolent — it looks ferocious/demonic but is genuinely harmless, even gentle. Nobody alive remembers this; the truth has been lost over generations, replaced by the "dangerous thing that must be appeased" story Sky-Bough believes today.
- The Fae was never bound out of fear — it was bound out of hunger. Sky-Bough's original settlers struggled against dangerous flora, fauna, and poor crop yields, until they found a grove and surrounding area that was inexplicably safe and fertile. They traced this to a nearby entry point into the Faewilds and a Fae creature that passed into the material realm from time to time. They bound the creature there, and the ritual sacrifice was the means by which they could hold it permanently and amplify its effect to cover a much larger area than its natural presence ever would have.
- The sacrifice is chosen by lot deliberately to diffuse responsibility: no family, faction, or leader can be blamed or accused of choosing scapegoats. It protects the village's social cohesion as much as it serves the ritual itself.
- The killing itself is entirely human-administered — the village carries it out directly, ritually, not the Fae. The victim's death is then drawn straight into the binding, their life-force becoming part of the wards that hold the Fae in place. The horror of the ritual is Sky-Bough's own doing, not something done to them by the creature they blame.
- Nobody alive knows the full true history. The mayor's office inherits the most complete version passed down, but that knowledge has been reshaped over generations to fit each era's mayor's own morality — it isn't a preserved record, it's an edited one. The only entity that actually remembers the truth is the bound Fae itself, but centuries of captivity have fragmented its memory and mind, so even it can't offer a clean account. See [[The Mayor]].
- Only adults born in Sky-Bough are eligible for the lottery, and only once they turn 25 — outsiders and children are never at risk.
- Last year's sacrifice was an ordinary villager in their early thirties, born and raised in Sky-Bough, with no scheme or plot behind their selection or escape — just an ordinary person who was afraid. They had recently fallen in love with a newer resident from Meridia, who didn't understand or accept the custom and begged them not to go through with it. That relationship, and the outsider's role in the escape, is part of why the mayor's growing circle of outsiders has become such a sore point among villagers since.
- The mayor presides over the sacrifice — it's one of the office's inherited duties, not something any mayor can opt out of. The general populace does not witness the ritual itself; only those required to administer it are present. See [[The Mayor]].
- The ritual can only be performed during a narrow annual window — a few minutes when the border between the Fae and material realms is at its thinnest — not at any time of the village's choosing. The exact details of the ritual (its timing, its location, its mechanics) are strictly guarded knowledge kept only among those native to Sky-Bough; outsiders are never told.
- The sacrifice had genuinely committed to going through with it and was not supposed to tell their lover anything — if they'd backed out early, the village simply would have chosen someone else, so the escape had to happen inside the ritual's live window itself. They broke that secrecy and told their lover anyway. The lover, on their own initiative and without a plan the sacrifice had agreed to, acted at the very last possible moment of the window: using flash/smoke grenades acquired through contacts in the Meridian Isles, thrown into the ritual site to blind and disorient everyone present just long enough that no substitute could be sacrificed in time. Nobody was hurt — the lover's only goal was saving their beloved, not harming anyone else.
- The two of them fled together immediately afterward, making for the Meridian Isles, but they haven't arrived yet — they're still making their way across Ilyndor. They're a findable, moving target if the party (or anyone else) goes looking.
- Neither of them knows anything the village doesn't already know. There was no secret plot or hidden knowledge behind the escape — the lover, as an outsider not raised in Sky-Bough's traditions, simply never believed the sacrifice was worth a life and refused to let their beloved die for what they saw as a sham. It was love and disbelief, not conspiracy.
- Nobody is actually hunting the escaped pair, and nothing supernatural is pulling them back either. As individuals they were never important — recovering them wouldn't undo the failure, since the ritual can only be completed during its narrow window, and the village can simply select someone else next year regardless. The mayor recognizes chasing them down would be a waste of effort against more pressing problems: the harvest failing from degrading soil quality, a rise in aggressive flora and fauna attacks near the village, and the fear that the imprisoned Fae's weakening bonds might eventually let it break free entirely.
- The fear of the Fae breaking free is justified, even though the Fae is gentle by nature. It is sentient and intelligent, not a beast — and centuries of imprisonment against its will have given festering hatred a long time to build. Gentle does not mean weak; a free Fae would be genuinely dangerous. What it would actually do if freed is not yet decided.
- The wards weakening doesn't just remove protection — it's actively leaking the Fae's own pent-up rage and grief from centuries of captivity into the surrounding land, undirected and uncontrolled. This is the actual mechanism behind both the souring harvest (soil turning infertile as that hatred seeps into it) and the rising aggression in local wildlife. See [[The Parkers and the Harvest]].
- The Fae's ambient magic had at least one more incidental effect nobody ever knew about: it had been keeping an ancient, unrelated fungal network dormant beneath Sky-Bough for centuries. This was never a deliberate function of the Pact, and the network's awakening as the wards fail is a coincidence of timing, not a consequence of the failed sacrifice itself. See [[The Missing Children]].
