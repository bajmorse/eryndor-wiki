# Session 1 GM Notes

GM-side record for Session 1. The player-facing account is [[Session 1]] and [[Session 1 Full Notes]].

## Continuity Flags

Three things said at the table sit awkwardly against established GM truths. None of them are visible to the players yet, so all three are still cheap to resolve either way — but they should be resolved deliberately rather than drifted past.

### 1. The tomb doors bear the mycelium mark

**What was said:** Zella's vision from the stone showed "two large stone doors in a muddy field beneath a tree, **with the same symbols on it as on the rock**."

**The conflict:** [[The Missing Children]] establishes that the disappearances have "no connection to the ancient tomb" and that the two threads are simultaneous "purely by chance." [[The Forgotten Hero and the Tomb]] likewise keeps the sealing entirely separate — different era, different power. If the tomb doors carry the Mycelium Host's mark, the two storylines are now joined at the most visible possible point.

**Options:**

- **Keep them separate.** The marks on the doors are *not* the same; the resemblance is the stone's influence colouring the vision, or an understandable misreading by Zella. Cheap to walk back, since only Zella saw it and she was mid-hallucination.
- **Join them deliberately.** The ancient culture that revered the Host built the ceremonial site somewhere in this forest, and the tomb was cut into or near it — the sorceress sealed her vessel in a structure that was already old and already marked. This is genuinely attractive: it explains why the tomb's architecture is strange enough to interest Mossback, and it gives the half-moon mark a reason to be near the farm. It does, however, mean the "unrelated by chance" line in two storylines needs rewriting.
- **Split the difference.** The doors carry a *different* ancient mark that merely resembles the half-moons, pointing at the same extinct culture without implicating the network itself.

### 2. Where the half-moon stone came from

**What was said:** Willy picked the stone up recently, "near that farm that everybody's talking about," where researchers had handled it and dropped it.

**The conflict:** [[Old Man Willy]] establishes the symbol-bearing object as an ancient ceremonial carved stone, "found by him, not traded, somewhere deep in the forest years ago."

**Cleanest reconciliation:** these are two different stones. The ancient ceremonial one is still somewhere in the collection, unseen — the party only searched the table — and what Willy showed off is a *fresh* mark from near the farm. That actually strengthens the existing canon rather than bending it: [[The Missing Children]] already has the network's reach "extending into nearby farmsteads," so a new mark appearing out by the farm is exactly what expanding reach looks like. It also means Frayne finding no match on the table is true but misleading.

### 3. Willy didn't act on wanting to confide

**What was said:** Willy treated the stone as nothing more than a pleasingly smooth rock, showed no flicker of recognition, and made no move to tell the party anything.

**The conflict:** [[Old Man Willy]] has him coming to the Bent Feather nightly specifically because he has recognised the symbol, knows where he found it, and wants to tell somebody — he just hasn't decided who to trust.

**Reading that preserves it:** he hasn't decided he trusts these five. They turned up on his doorstep, disarmed him, pickpocketed him, and interrogated him about sacrificing children; that is not the conversation he was waiting for. The thread is intact and still unplayed. Worth giving him a deliberate opening next time he appears — he is bad at starting conversations, so he will need one offered to him.

## Threads the Session Opened

- **Frayne is the closers' figurehead.** Unintended, and entirely the product of one Presence roll. The hardline core left the platform promising consequences, and they consider Frayne one of theirs. He is also now complicit in killing two of them, which the survivors know.
- **Two dead villagers, two witnesses.** The Kenku bruiser flew off and is at large; the Kenku woman was left alive with the bodies. Neither has any reason to be quiet. The party committed a double killing in a fistfight, in the upper boroughs, in daylight, having been publicly identified that morning. There is no version of this that stays private.
- **The dead Katari's daughter is alive.** Per [[The Missing Children]], she is walking around Sky-Bough unrecognised. The party is carrying her drawing. If they ever cross her — or show the drawing to the right person — that is the cleanest possible route into the real nature of the disappearances, and it is now attached to a man they killed.
- **Mossback floated the children being in the tomb.** False, per canon, but it is now a publicly stated theory from a respected scholar. When the tomb turns out not to contain them, that costs him credibility and sends the village looking elsewhere — probably back at Willy.
- **Aangry's longing for the door.** Improvised at the table and worth keeping. He has an unexplained personal pull toward the tomb, which no one has a reason for yet.
- **The pressure in the stone.** Zella felt something trying to get in and instinctively refused it. Per canon that is spore-based implantation, and per canon it only takes on children — so an adult brushing it off is correct. Note that she licked it, which is about as close to direct contact as it gets.

## Consequences to Track

- Who finds the bodies, and how fast word spreads.
- Whether the surviving Kenku names Frayne specifically, given the appeal made to him on the bridge.
- Whether the remaining hardliners escalate at the tomb itself at noon, now that two of their number are dead.
- Willy's answer on whether he comes along.
- The half-moon stone is still in Willy's house, not in the party's hands.

## Mechanical Notes

- **Nine Fear banked** going into the tomb. The bridge fight was run with that as its purpose.
- Frayne took a Slayer die rather than Hope on his opening Presence roll.
- Nyx was the only character to fail the Instinct roll to spot Willy, and so missed the whole approach.
- The loot rolled off the Katari's body included some items from a mis-set loot table (quivers, an arcane shard) which were explicitly retracted at the table. The real haul is two gold coins, crushed herbs, and the drawing.

## Table Notes

- **Decision procedure established.** At branching points, every player picks an option, and the outcome is then chosen at random *weighted by the votes* — so a 3–1–1 split is a 60/20/20 chance rather than a guaranteed win for the majority. The tomb came up this way. Worth keeping consistent, since the players liked it and it preserves genuine uncertainty.
- **Finnegan's persona was settled by a coin flip** mid-session: Mike chose to play him as a pompous, self-important chronicler who narrates his own note-taking and dictates to an absent scribe. Expect him to document everything and to be reliably unhelpful in a fight.
- The village's rumour mill was played for laughs as a tabloid news parody. The substance underneath is usable and in-world: a widely circulated interview in which Willy said he would "teach those darn children a lesson one day," plus the detail that two of the five children seen carving stones that day are now missing. Keep the substance, drop the framing.
- Running jokes that stuck: Old Man Willy did nothing wrong; smooth rock ratings out of ten; crows being called owls; "counterpoint: money."
